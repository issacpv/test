# Leakage, scorers and age shift: an audit of scalp-EEG seizure detectors trained on CHB-MIT and transferred to adult and neonatal cohorts

**Background.** Scalp-EEG seizure detectors routinely report above 95% performance on CHB-MIT, yet the 2025 SzCORE challenge reached an event-level F1 of 0.43 on continuous EEG. Random window splits leak subject identity, and cross-cohort benchmarks of EEG foundation models document large drops between cohorts without explaining them. Nobody has quantified leakage inflation from window-wise to cross-cohort evaluation for the same model families under one event-based scorer, tested whether model rankings depend on the scorer, separated montage and sampling-rate shift from age and etiology shift, or evaluated label-free domain adaptation with false alarms per day as the endpoint.

**Objective.** To audit how much CHB-MIT performance is inflated by split regime and model capacity, whether scorer choice reorders models, how much of the pediatric-to-adult and pediatric-to-neonatal drop is acquisition versus age shift, and whether foundation models and domain adaptation close the gap.

**Methods.** CHB-MIT (23 pediatric subjects, about 980 hours, about 198 seizures) is the training source; Siena (14 adults, 47 seizures), TUSZ v2.0.3 (about 1,600 hours, mixed ages) and the Helsinki neonatal corpus (79 neonates, three annotators) are zero-shot targets. All EDFs will be harmonised to an 18-pair longitudinal bipolar montage at 256 Hz. Model families are spectral features with logistic regression, Riemannian tangent-space logistic regression, a compact CNN, and BIOT, LaBraM, EEGPT and CBraMod as linear probes and full fine-tunes. Four CHB-MIT split regimes (window-wise, record-wise, patient-wise, leave-one-patient-out) precede cross-cohort evaluation, with thresholds and post-processing frozen on CHB-MIT validation folds and target labels used only for scoring. Every model is scored with OVLP, TAES and SzCORE, reporting SzCORE F1 and sensitivity at 1, 5 and 10 false positives per 24 hours as primary endpoints, with 2,000-draw subject-level bootstrap CIs, a mixed model for the model-family by split-regime interaction, Kendall's tau between scorer rankings, Spearman correlation of log-MMD between background windows and F1 drop, label-shuffled nulls and Holm correction within hypothesis families. Riemannian re-centering, Euclidean alignment, CORAL and AdaBN will use unlabelled target background only; cells with pretraining overlap on TUH corpora are flagged.

**Expected results.** We expect record-wise minus patient-wise inflation above 0.15 F1 for CNN and foundation-model families and below 0.10 for the spectral baseline, scorer rank correlations below 0.8 on at least one cohort, harmonisation halving the Siena drop but not the Helsinki drop, log-MMD correlating above 0.6 with loss, fine-tuned foundation models reducing but not removing the drop, and re-centering helping on adult targets while raising neonatal false alarms.

**Significance.** The result is a fixed-split, fixed-scorer cross-dataset leaderboard that any detector can enter, and evidence about which reported gains reflect memorised patient identity rather than seizure detection.
