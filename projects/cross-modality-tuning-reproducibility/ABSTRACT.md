# Decomposing the two-photon versus Neuropixels tuning gap into sampling, measurement and analysis with the Allen Brain Observatory

**Background.** Calcium imaging and extracellular electrophysiology both support quantitative claims about visual tuning, yet the Allen Institute's matched-stimulus comparison found that stimulus preferences agree across modalities while responsiveness and selectivity differ, with a spike-to-fluorescence forward model reducing but not removing the gap. The relative contributions of which cells are sampled (layer, cell class, firing rate), how activity is measured (indicator kinetics, nonlinearity, deconvolution) and how tuning is analysed (metric definitions, inclusion criteria) have never been estimated in one framework, cross-modality agreement has not been compared with within-modality test-retest reliability, and the Visual Behavior datasets, which repeat the two-modality design in an active task, have not been used to test whether the Visual Coding conclusions transfer.

**Objective.** To decompose the modality gap in tuning statistics into sampling, measurement and analysis terms with a reliability ceiling, test whether the decomposition transfers from passive viewing to an active task, and identify modality-invariant metrics under pre-registered criteria.

**Methods.** From Allen Visual Coding 2-photon (about 1,300 experiments, about 60,000 neurons, 14 Cre lines) and Visual Coding Neuropixels (58 sessions, about 40,000 units), we will compute trial-by-condition responses in three 2-photon signal versions (dF/F, Allen events, AR(1)-deconvolved rates) and as spike counts, deriving responsiveness, OSI/DSI, preferred direction, TF and SF, and lifetime sparseness. Propensity weights on layer, cell-class proxy and firing-rate proxy will estimate the sampling term. A GCaMP6f/6s forward model with supralinear amplification and saturation applied to Neuropixels spike trains, followed by the identical 2-photon pipeline, will estimate the measurement term; a factorial multiverse of signal type, neuropil rule, ROI QC, ephys curation, metric and response window will estimate the analysis term. The gap will be attributed sequentially, averaged over all six orderings with bootstrap CIs. Split-half, across-session and across-block reliabilities will disattenuate per-area agreement. Distribution differences use Wasserstein distance against within-modality bootstrap nulls, mixed models carry mouse random effects, and forward-model parameters and invariance thresholds fixed on Visual Coding will be applied unchanged to Visual Behavior 2-photon and Neuropixels (about 150 sessions), with Holm correction within families and linear-kernel forward-model nulls.

**Expected results.** We expect preference distributions to agree within the null in both paradigms while magnitude distributions differ; sampling reweighting and forward modelling to each close at least 30% of the responsiveness gap with nonlinearity parameters transferring within 20%; fraction-selective claims to vary by over 15 percentage points across the multiverse; disattenuated agreement above 0.8 for preferences and 0.5 for magnitudes; and reliability-normalised, deconvolution-based metrics to meet the 0.2 SD invariance criterion.

**Significance.** The study would turn a documented discrepancy into an attributed budget, with a checklist of tuning statistics that survive a change of recording modality and a harmonised cross-modality tuning table.
