# Does the pulse-arrival-time to blood-pressure calibration shift in pregnancy? A transportability audit of cuffless BP models on open ICU waveform records

**Background.** Cuffless blood-pressure estimation from pulse arrival time (PAT) and PPG morphology rests on the Bramwell-Hill relation between arterial stiffness and pressure, so every estimator embeds its training population's compliance. Pregnancy raises arterial compliance; preeclampsia partly reverses this. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) affect 5-10% of pregnancies, yet no open-data study has asked whether PAT-SBP calibration differs in pregnant or HDP patients.

**Objective.** To quantify the pregnancy shift of PAT-SBP calibration, test whether the slope steepens in HDP, and audit whether general-population cuffless models transfer to pregnant patients under ISO 81060-2 criteria.

**Methods.** We will label pregnant, HDP and matched non-pregnant female records (age 18-45, 1:3 on age band and care unit) in the MIMIC-III Waveform Matched Subset (10,282 patients) and the MIMIC-IV Waveform Database from ICD codes and obstetric care units. Beat-level PAT, proximal PAT (R-to-ABP-foot) will be extracted with template-correlation quality control. Linear mixed models of SBP on PAT x group with a heart-rate covariate and random subject intercepts and slopes will estimate intercept and slope shifts. Primary hypotheses (>= 5 mmHg pregnancy intercept shift; >= 20% steeper HDP slope; ISO criterion 1 failure of PulseDB- and VitalDB-trained models with bias-dominated error) will be Holm-corrected, with subject-cluster bootstrap, group-label permutation and a within-subject time-shuffled PAT null. PulseDB subjects overlapping our cohorts are excluded from training and calibration beats never evaluated. Because obstetric ICU admissions are rare and VitalDB may contain no caesarean cases, a month-2 stop/go rule applies: with fewer than 30 pregnant records holding 30 min of clean data, the study is re-scoped as a methods, feasibility and mechanistic-model paper, and VitalDB serves only as the anaesthetised non-pregnant reference.

**Expected results.** We expect a positive intercept shift in normotensive pregnancy, a steeper slope with negative intercept in HDP, bias-dominated transfer failure that one-point calibration removes in normotensive pregnancy but not HDP, and an exponential-elastance model with pregnancy-specific stiffness reproducing the sign and magnitude of these shifts.

**Significance.** This would be the first open-data quantification of cuffless BP transportability in pregnancy, released with a beat-level pipeline and a mechanistic explanation, informing validation of devices used at the 140/90 decision threshold.
