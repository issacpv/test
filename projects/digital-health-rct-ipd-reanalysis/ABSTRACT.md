# Engagement is not a dose: a registry audit of IPD retrievability and a pre-registered individual-participant re-analysis of digital-health randomised trials

**Background.** Across 176 RCTs, mental-health apps show small-to-medium pooled effects, and attrition is the field's defining problem. Engagement is read as the dose, but engagers self-select, so within-arm correlations cannot be read causally, and most trials report MAR-based analyses whose sensitivity to informative dropout is unknown. IPD meta-analyses of internet CBT pool collaborator data and treat adherence descriptively; no one has measured how much digital-trial IPD is retrievable or applied principal-stratum and reference-based imputation methods across platform-hosted trials.

**Objective.** To quantify the retrievability of digital-health RCT IPD and, in the trials obtained, estimate causal engagement effects, attrition sensitivity and transportable treatment-effect heterogeneity under one pre-registered protocol.

**Methods.** The registry arm will query the ClinicalTrials.gov API for completed digital-intervention RCTs registered since 2015 with >= 100 participants, extract IPD-sharing statements, verify each NCT id on the named platform, and model IPD = Yes on sponsor class, enrolment and year. This arm uses only public metadata and is publishable on its own. Because platform IPD (Vivli, YODA, CSDR, NIMH Data Archive) requires applications taking 6-12 months and may be refused, applications run in parallel, open-repository and NDA trials form a guaranteed analysis set, and the analysis plan is registered before any IPD arrives. Per trial we will estimate ITT, naive per-protocol contrasts, Wald CACE and principal-score-weighted engager and dose-tertile effects with cluster bootstrap; ITT under MAR, jump-to-reference and delta-adjusted imputation with tipping points; and baseline-severity moderation validated by internal-external cross-validation, pooled by two-stage random-effects meta-analysis. Nulls are within-trial permutation of allocation and simulated trials with zero engagement effect and known MNAR dropout, which must return unbiased principal-score estimates while naive contrasts stay biased.

**Expected results.** We expect fewer than 25% of eligible trials to state IPD sharing and fewer than half of those to be findable, engager effects attenuated by at least 50% relative to naive contrasts, a dose plateau, one third of significant ITT effects lost under jump-to-reference, and baseline severity as the only moderator whose calibration slope excludes zero.

**Significance.** The programme turns data-access failure into a measured outcome and replaces correlational engagement claims with randomisation-respecting estimands.
