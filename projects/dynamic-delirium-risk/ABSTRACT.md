# How Much of Dynamic ICU Delirium Risk Is the Sedation Plan? Quantifying Sedation-Policy Leakage and Informative Non-Assessment in Landmark Models on MIMIC-IV and eICU

**Background.** Dynamic ICU delirium models updated every 12 h from EHR time series report high discrimination, and a 2025 mixture-of-experts model predicts delirium and coma jointly, conditioned on current brain status, with external validation. Yet every such model uses sedative doses and RASS values as predictors although they are partly treatment decisions, and treats unscreened windows as ignorable. Deeper sedation lowers the label rate, so sedatives look protective, and a high score may reflect a plan to lighten sedation. No study has measured how much of the gain comes from sedation-policy features or from who is screened.

**Objective.** We will quantify sedation-policy leakage and informative non-assessment in dynamic delirium prediction, test how coding of comatose and unscreened windows changes discrimination and sedative coefficients, and test MIMIC-IV to eICU-CRD transportability conditional on screening density.

**Methods.** From MIMIC-IV v3.1 (about 94,000 ICU stays) we will parse CAM-ICU and RASS items into 12-h window states and build landmark datasets under three label schemes: multi-state (normal, delirium, coma-unassessable, unscreened, discharged, dead), binary with coma/unscreened excluded, and binary with coma coded negative. Logistic, multinomial and LightGBM landmark models will use physiology, sedation-policy and assessment-process feature blocks with leave-block-out ablation; transition-intensity Poisson regressions will estimate sedative-specific effects; a screening model supplies inverse-probability-of-assessment weights. After subject-level and temporal validation, the frozen model is applied to eICU-CRD (about 200,000 stays, 208 hospitals) with per-hospital recalibration. Cluster bootstrap over patients, a permuted-window-order control and a null simulator with no true sedation effect provide inference.

**Expected results.** We expect the label scheme to shift AUROC of identical features by at least 0.05 and flip the sign of a sedative coefficient; removing sedation-policy features to erase at least 30% of the gain over an admission-only model; benzodiazepines but not dexmedetomidine to raise delirium transition intensities; a prior positive screen to raise assessment odds above 1.5; and the largest external loss in the lowest screening-density tertile of eICU hospitals, with wider calibration gaps by age, sex and race under binary-negative coding.

**Significance.** The study separates delirium risk from knowledge of sedation plans and screening behaviour, yielding lower but transportable discrimination and sedative effects consistent with trials.
