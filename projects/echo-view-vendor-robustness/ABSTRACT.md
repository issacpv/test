# Echocardiographic View Classification Under Scanner-Vendor Shift: A Leave-One-Source-Out Benchmark with Acquisition Decomposition and Clinically Weighted Confusions

**Background.** Every echocardiographic measurement is view-specific, so a view classifier that silently mislabels views on a new scanner corrupts ejection fraction and strain. View models are usually trained and tested on one institution and one vendor. A 2026 six-dataset study of left-ventricular segmentation showed that echo domain shift is largely field-of-view and framing rather than intensity, and that apparent vendor effects are confounded with dataset; view benchmarks such as TTE47 evaluate methods within one source. No study transfers that decomposition to view classification, maps which acquisition differences induce dangerous confusions, or compares label-free adaptation on a common shift grid.

**Objective.** We will measure per-view degradation of view classifiers across public sources that differ in vendor, site and format, decompose it into sector geometry, frame rate, intensity and residual source effects with controlled counterfactual renderings, characterise clinically consequential confusions, compare label-free adaptation strategies, and quantify downstream EF error.

**Methods.** Frames from EchoNet-Dynamic, CAMUS, TMED-2, TTE47 and a view-labelled sample of MIMIC-IV-ECHO (Philips/GE DICOM) will be sector-masked, resized and mapped to a canonical view set. A handcrafted-feature logistic baseline, a 2D CNN and frozen echo-foundation-model features with a linear head will be evaluated on a leave-one-source-out grid with study-level splits. Histogram matching, sector re-masking, frame-rate resampling and gamma jitter are counterfactual transforms whose Shapley-averaged contributions decompose the macro-F1 drop; a clinically weighted error up-weights A4C/A2C and apical/parasternal swaps. Instance normalisation, test-time BatchNorm adaptation and histogram matching will be compared without target labels. Nulls are a shuffled-source test and a permutation confusion-matrix null; study-level bootstrap and Benjamini-Hochberg correction provide inference.

**Expected results.** We expect the largest drops for A4C versus A2C and PSAX levels, with source and vendor effects partly separable only where sources share a vendor; geometry matching to remove more of the drop than intensity matching for coarse views, as reported for segmentation; cross-vendor confusion mass to concentrate on dangerous swaps so the weighted error widens more than raw accuracy; frozen foundation features with instance normalisation to give the best label-free recovery; and realistic confusion rates to inflate downstream EF MAE measurably.

**Significance.** The benchmark and decomposition tell developers which acquisition differences to normalise and where a view classifier must abstain before it can be trusted across scanners.
