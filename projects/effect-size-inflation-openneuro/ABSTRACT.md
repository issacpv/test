# How inflated are the effect sizes reported for OpenNeuro datasets? A repository-anchored winner's-curse audit with cross-validated re-analysis

**Background.** Published neuroimaging effects are inflated by selection across studies, since small studies reach significance only when the estimate is large, and by selection within studies at the peak voxel chosen because it was largest. OpenNeuro datasets have a median of 23 participants, and brain-wide association work has quantified inflation by subsampling large cohorts, but no study has used the literature as the unit of analysis with exact sample sizes, applied selection-corrected estimators to task-activation effects, or measured both mechanisms on the same data.

**Objective.** To join every OpenNeuro dataset's sample size to the peak effect its linked paper reports, estimate selection-corrected effects and replication expectations, and measure within-study circularity by cross-validated re-analysis.

**Methods.** The OpenNeuro GraphQL API (~1,300 datasets) will supply n from the BIDS snapshot, tasks and linked DOIs, with GitHub mirrors as fallback. Two annotators will extract from open-access papers the primary contrast, peak statistic, threshold type and whether the peak was chosen a priori, with kappa on 50 papers; statistics are converted to Cohen's d. Corrected effects come from a truncated-normal selection model at each study's own threshold: Type M and Type S errors, conditional MLE with profile-likelihood CIs and empirical Bayes by task family, validated on simulated literatures matched to the corpus's n and threshold mix. In 20-30 task-fMRI datasets with fMRIPrep derivatives and n >= 16, first-level GLMs will yield the in-sample peak d and a K-fold cross-validated peak d with selection and estimation on disjoint subjects, compared with the paper's value by Wilcoxon tests on log ratios. Nulls are label-permuted contrasts, whose circular-to-cross-validated ratio must follow the null distribution of the maximum, and a synthetic no-selection literature returning unbiased effects. H1-H4 are pre-registered and Holm-corrected; the metadata arm is laptop-scale and publishable alone if re-analysis is delayed.

**Expected results.** We expect reported |d| to fall with n (Spearman rho < -0.3) with a positive Egger intercept, corrected effects below 60% of reported values at n <= 25 and above 85% at n >= 100, circular-to-cross-validated ratios above 1.5 at n <= 25, median replication probability below 0.5 at the original n, and larger inflation for cognitive than sensorimotor contrasts.

**Significance.** The corpus gives a calibrated prior on how much small-n task-fMRI effects shrink, separating publication selection from circularity.
