# Where and why ICU prediction models break: decomposing discrimination loss into covariate, label and concept shift, with recalibration label budgets, across MIMIC-IV, eICU-CRD, HiRID and AmsterdamUMCdb

**Background.** Every major open ICU database now has a benchmark, and cross-database drops for sepsis, kidney injury and mortality models are well documented. Recent work has begun to explain the calibration side of the gap: prevalence-driven label shift accounts for roughly half to two thirds of calibration error when a MIMIC-IV mortality model is deployed across 185 eICU hospitals, intercept-only correction repairs much of it, and a four-database validation of sepsis models has charted subgroup discrimination and calibration slope by age, sex and race. What remains unexplained is the discrimination loss: no study attributes it to covariate, label or concept shift for each ordered site pair, no task other than mortality or sepsis has been examined this way, and the number of labelled target stays that each recalibration method needs, overall and per subgroup, is unknown.

**Objective.** We will decompose the cross-site loss in discrimination and calibration of sepsis, acute kidney injury and 48-hour mortality models into covariate, label and concept components for every ordered site pair, and measure how many labelled target stays intercept, slope and non-parametric recalibration need to restore calibration, pooled and within subgroups.

**Methods.** MIMIC-IV v3.1 (about 94,000 ICU stays), eICU-CRD v2.0 (about 200,000), HiRID v1.1.1 (about 34,000) and AmsterdamUMCdb v1.0.2 (about 23,000) will be mapped onto a 38-concept ontology whose ricu and YAIB ids are verified against each database's item dictionary. YAIB-compatible cohorts (adults, first stay, prediction at 24 hours) carry 48-hour mortality, KDIGO AKI and Sepsis-3 labels. Logistic regression and LightGBM models tuned by patient-grouped cross-validation are evaluated on a source hold-out and on each other site. Covariate shift is estimated by importance-weighted source evaluation from a domain classifier, label shift by black-box shift estimation, and concept shift as the remainder, with Shapley averaging over component orderings and bootstrap intervals, applied to AUROC, calibration intercept and slope separately. Intercept-only, Platt, temperature and isotonic recalibration is evaluated for 25 to 2,000 target stays, pooled and group-wise by sex, age band and, in US databases, race, with permutation nulls. A negative control decomposes two halves of one site; a positive control recovers an induced shift; a prevalence-only correction using the local outcome rate serves as the zero-label baseline.

**Expected results.** We expect concept shift to dominate sepsis discrimination loss and covariate shift the AKI loss, with label shift contributing little to AUROC but most of the intercept drift across all three tasks; about 100 labelled stays to remove at least 80 percent of calibration-in-the-large error beyond the prevalence-only baseline, slope needing 500 and isotonic 2,000; and group-wise recalibration to be required where subgroup calibration gaps widen externally.

**Significance.** A per-task, per-site-pair table of shift components and recalibration budgets tells hospitals what a published model needs before deployment.
