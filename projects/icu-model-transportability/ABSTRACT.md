# Where and why ICU prediction models break: shift decomposition, calibration and subgroup parity across MIMIC-IV, eICU-CRD, HiRID and AmsterdamUMCdb

**Background.** Every major open ICU database now has a benchmark, and cross-database performance drops for sepsis, kidney injury and mortality models are well documented. Transfer studies report a single discrimination delta and stop: the gap is not attributed to covariate, label or concept shift, calibration is seldom reported although it governs alerts, subgroup performance at the new site is rarely examined, and the cost of restoring calibration with local labels is unquantified. The closest multi-centre sepsis work covers three databases without decomposing the loss.

**Objective.** We will decompose the cross-site gap of sepsis, acute kidney injury and 48-hour mortality models into covariate, label and concept components for every ordered site pair, characterise calibration and subgroup parity at external sites, and measure how many labelled target stays few-shot recalibration needs.

**Methods.** MIMIC-IV v3.1 (about 94,000 ICU stays), eICU-CRD v2.0 (about 200,000), HiRID v1.1.1 (about 34,000) and AmsterdamUMCdb v1.0.2 (about 23,000) will be extracted with DuckDB onto a 38-concept shared ontology whose ricu and YAIB ids are verified programmatically against each database's item dictionary. YAIB-compatible cohorts (adults, first stay, prediction at 24 hours) carry labels for 48-hour mortality, KDIGO stage 1 or higher AKI and Sepsis-3 onset. Logistic regression and LightGBM models tuned by patient-grouped cross-validation on the source site are evaluated on a 20 percent source hold-out and on each other site. Covariate shift is estimated by importance-weighted source evaluation from a domain classifier, label shift by black-box shift estimation, and concept shift as the remainder, with Shapley averaging over orderings and bootstrap intervals. Calibration is reported as intercept, slope, ECE, Brier decomposition and net benefit; parity by sex, age band and, in the US databases, race, with permutation nulls. Few-shot intercept-only, Platt, temperature and isotonic recalibration is evaluated for 25 to 2,000 target stays over 200 repeats, pooled and group-wise. Negative controls decompose two halves of one site; a positive control recovers an induced label shift.

**Expected results.** We expect label shift to dominate calibration-intercept drift, with intercepts tracking the log prevalence ratio (rho above 0.7); concept shift to dominate sepsis discrimination loss and covariate shift the AKI loss; slopes below 1 at every external site; subgroup calibration gaps to widen externally and persist after label-shift correction; about 100 labelled stays to remove at least 80 percent of calibration-in-the-large error, with slope needing 500 and isotonic 2,000; and pooled recalibration to leave subgroup gaps that group-wise recalibration closes.

**Significance.** A per-task, per-site-pair table of shift components and recalibration budgets tells hospitals what a published model needs before it is safe to deploy locally.
