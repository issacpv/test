# Where and why ICU prediction models break: decomposing discrimination loss into covariate, label and concept shift, with recalibration label budgets, across MIMIC-IV, eICU-CRD, HiRID and AmsterdamUMCdb

**Background.** Cross-database drops for ICU models are well documented, and the calibration side is partly explained: prevalence-driven label shift accounts for half to two thirds of calibration error when a MIMIC-IV mortality model meets 185 eICU hospitals, intercept-only correction repairs much of it, and a four-database validation of sepsis models has charted subgroup AUROC and calibration slope. The discrimination loss remains unexplained: no study attributes it to covariate, label or concept shift per site pair, kidney injury has not been examined, and the labelled target stays each recalibration method needs, overall and per subgroup, are unknown.

**Objective.** We will decompose the cross-site loss in discrimination and calibration of sepsis, acute kidney injury and 48-hour mortality models into covariate, label and concept components for every ordered site pair, and measure how many labelled target stays intercept, slope and non-parametric recalibration need, pooled and within subgroups.

**Methods.** MIMIC-IV v3.1 (about 94,000 ICU stays), eICU-CRD v2.0 (about 200,000), HiRID v1.1.1 (about 34,000) and AmsterdamUMCdb v1.0.2 (about 23,000) are mapped onto a verified 38-concept ontology with ricu and YAIB ids. YAIB-compatible cohorts (adults, first stay, prediction at 24 hours) carry 48-hour mortality, KDIGO AKI and Sepsis-3 labels. Logistic regression and LightGBM are evaluated on a source hold-out and every other site. Covariate shift is estimated by importance-weighted source evaluation, label shift by black-box shift estimation and concept shift as the remainder, Shapley-averaged with bootstrap intervals, separately for AUROC, intercept and slope. Intercept-only, Platt, temperature and isotonic recalibration is evaluated for 25 to 2,000 target stays, pooled and by sex, age band and, in US databases, race. A negative control decomposes two halves of one site, a positive control recovers an induced shift, and prevalence-only correction is the zero-label baseline.

**Expected results.** We expect concept shift to dominate sepsis discrimination loss and covariate shift the AKI loss, label shift driving intercept drift but not AUROC; about 100 labelled stays to remove at least 80 percent of calibration-in-the-large error beyond the prevalence-only baseline, slope needing 500 and isotonic 2,000; and group-wise recalibration to be required where subgroup calibration gaps widen externally.

**Significance.** A per-task, per-site-pair table of shift components and recalibration budgets tells hospitals what a published model needs before deployment.
