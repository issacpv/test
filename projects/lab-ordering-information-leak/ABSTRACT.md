# How Much of ICU Outcome Prediction Is the Clinician's Ordering Behaviour? Attributing, Localising and Stress-Testing the Laboratory Ordering Channel in MIMIC-IV and eICU

**Background.** ICU labs are ordered when clinicians worry, so order presence, timing and urgency partly encode the clinician's forecast. Missingness-only models reach 84-93% of value-only discrimination in MIMIC-IV, ordering-pattern models rival value models in eICU, measurement counts hurt MIMIC-to-eICU transfer, and missingness indicators weakly predict race. Missing are an exact attribution of how much benchmark performance is ordering rather than values, an anatomy of where it lives (routine, STAT, off-schedule, ordered-but-unresulted), degradation under policy shift as a function of mask reliance, and a test of order-agnostic training.

**Objective.** We will compute the share of predictive information attributable to ordering versus values, localise it by order type, relate mask reliance to degradation under cross-hospital, cross-era and thinning shifts, compare order-agnostic training strategies, and test whether the mask channel encodes insurance and language beyond the weak race signal already reported.

**Methods.** In MIMIC-IV v3.1 (~94k ICU stays; lab priority, order and result times) and eICU-CRD v2.0 (~200k stays, 208 hospitals), adults on a first ICU stay of >= 24 h are predicted at 24 h for 48-h and in-hospital mortality, 48-h AKI and ICU LOS > 7 days. Value and mask tables over 26 labs feed logistic regression and boosted trees; four fits per fold give an exact two-player Shapley split of held-out log-likelihood gain, eight localise the mask share. Shift experiments train on eICU ordering-intensity tertiles and MIMIC-IV 2008-2013 eras and test elsewhere, thinning test orders to 10%. Order dropout, schedule standardisation, intensity reweighting and a joint observation-process model are compared on internal cost versus external gain. Validation uses subject- and hospital-grouped splits, bootstrap CIs and Benjamini-Hochberg correction; controls are within-stay permutation of order times, mask permutation across count-matched stays and a semi-synthetic generator with known informativeness.

**Expected results.** We expect at least 25% of the 48-h mortality gain to sit in the ordering channel, concentrated in STAT and off-schedule orders in the last 6 h; AUROC drops under shift correlating with mask share (Spearman rho > 0.6); order dropout halving degradation at <= 0.01 internal AUROC cost; and mask-only models predicting insurance and language with AUROC above 0.60.

**Significance.** A per-task table of ordering-channel shares and a validated order-agnostic recipe give ICU modelling groups a leak budget and a transfer-robust default.
