# Graph Neural Networks on Dendritic Trees Predict Intrinsic Electrophysiology and Transfer From Mouse to Human

**Background.** The dependence of firing on dendritic shape is a classic structure–function question, and the Allen Cell Types Database now pairs standardised patch-clamp features with SWC reconstructions and, through Patch-seq, transcriptomic types for mouse and human cells. Existing predictive work uses hand-crafted morphometrics on mouse cells only, graph representations of morphology are evaluated on cell-type classification rather than regression, cross-species transfer has been shown only for electrophysiology-to-transcriptomics mapping, and biophysical models have never served as a predictive baseline against learned models.

**Objective.** We will test whether a graph neural network on the SWC tree regresses intrinsic electrophysiological features, whether the mapping carries information within transcriptomic types, whether it transports from mouse to human, and whether it beats type-average biophysical models simulated on each cell's own morphology.

**Methods.** We will assemble on the order of 2,000 mouse and 400 human Allen cells with electrophysiology, several hundred of each with reconstructions, joining input resistance, sag, time constant, rheobase, upstroke/downstroke ratio, adaptation and f-I slope, with AP half-width recomputed from NWB sweeps and validated by Bland–Altman. Each reconstruction becomes a graph with 15 node features; a four-layer GraphSAGE regressor with Huber loss and five-seed ensembles will be trained with rotation, jitter and subtree-dropout augmentation, optionally pre-trained self-supervised on overlap-filtered NeuroMorpho graphs. Baselines are ridge on 25 morphometrics, a size-only model and a type-mean model, all under GroupKFold by donor or by animal and Cre line with inner-fold tuning. Transfer is evaluated zero-shot and with head fine-tuning on 10, 25 and 50 human cells over 20 random subsets; the biophysical baseline simulates the long-square protocol in NEURON with type-average parameters on the target morphology. Label permutation (500 per target), 1,000 cell-level bootstraps and Benjamini–Hochberg correction support inference.

**Expected results.** We expect out-of-fold R² above 0.3 for passive features, exceeding ridge by more than 0.05, with active features below 0.2; within-type partial R² above 0.1 for passive features only; zero-shot retention of more than half the within-species R² for input resistance and time constant after size normalisation but not for sag or AP width; biophysical models matching the GNN on mouse yet transferring worse to human; and seed-stable attributions on proximal dendrites.

**Significance.** A validated morphology-to-electrophysiology mapping with stated transfer limits would let electrophysiology be imputed for reconstruction-only and EM-derived cells and show which properties of human neurons are geometric rather than channel-driven.
