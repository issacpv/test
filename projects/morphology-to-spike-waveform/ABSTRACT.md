# Dendritic Morphology Versus Channel Composition as Determinants of Extracellular Spike Waveforms on High-Density Probes

**Background.** Extracellular action potential shape depends on transmembrane currents and on the morphology that carries return currents, and high-density probes now record spatiotemporal footprints across tens of sites. Waveform classifiers trained on opto-tagged units reach high accuracy but cannot explain why a class has its footprint, and many opto-tagged interneurons are not narrow-spiking. Simulation studies showing that morphology shapes the waveform have used a few morphologies and a single probe, so no population-scale variance partition between morphology and channel composition exists.

**Objective.** We will quantify, for each waveform feature, the variance attributable to dendritic morphology versus ion-channel densities, identify which morphological features carry it, test whether the answer depends on probe geometry, validate simulated class footprints against opto-tagged in-vivo units, and derive morphology-conditioned waveform priors.

**Methods.** Using roughly 600 mouse and 150 human Allen Cell Types reconstructions with perisomatic and all-active models, and the 9,200 all-active model ensembles for 230 cells, we will run a factorial design: each morphology under its own fitted model, under channel sets transplanted from same- and different-class cells, and each channel set on other morphologies, dropping transplants that fail to spike. NEURON compartment currents will be projected through a line-source forward model onto Neuropixels 1.0, 2.0 and Ultra geometries over a placement grid of 10–100 µm soma-to-shank distance, depth and rotation. Features include trough-to-peak, half-width, repolarisation slope, footprint, decay exponent, above/below asymmetry and propagation velocity. A sum-of-squares decomposition with bootstrap CIs over cells yields eta² for morphology, channels, interaction and placement; ridge and gradient-boosted regression from morphology to waveform features use grouped five-fold CV by cell with a shuffled-feature negative control. Simulated class distributions will be compared with opto-tagged Pvalb, Sst and Vip units from Allen Visual Coding and Neuropixels Ultra recordings by energy distance and permutation tests, with BH-FDR across features and sensitivity to conductivity and shank boundary.

**Expected results.** We expect spatial features to be more than 60% morphology-driven and temporal features more than 60% channel-driven, dendritic surface area within 100 µm and stem count to predict footprint with cross-validated R² above 0.5, morphology-explained fractions to rise from NP1.0 to NP Ultra, simulated footprint medians to match opto-tagged medians within 10 µm, and priors to add at least five points of opto-tagged class accuracy.

**Significance.** A per-probe lookup table from morphology to waveform would give Neuropixels laboratories mechanistic priors for cell-type identification and a plausibility check on sorted units, explaining broad-spiking interneurons from geometry rather than channels.
