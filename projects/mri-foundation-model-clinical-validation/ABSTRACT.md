# Do Brain-MRI Foundation Models Survive Clinical Data? A Quality- and Pathology-Stratified External Validation

**Background.** Brain-MRI foundation models such as BrainSegFounder, SAM-Med3D and related encoders promise segmentation without task-specific training, but their reported gains come from curated, skull-stripped, quality-filtered challenge sets, and the one community evaluation on clinical out-of-distribution data keeps its test set private. Robustness to artefact has been studied for classical pipelines and image-enhancement models, not for segmentation foundation models, and none has been evaluated on paired scans with and without deliberate head motion.

**Objective.** We will build an open, reproducible benchmark that reports foundation-model performance as a function of measured image quality, lesion load, age and prompt regime against task-specific nnU-Net and contrast-agnostic SynthSeg, and locate the label budget at which a from-scratch model catches up.

**Methods.** We will harmonise MR-ART (148 adults, three motion levels each), MindBoggle-101, CANDI (103 children), Hammers (30 adults), ATLAS v2.0 (655 public stroke masks), ISLES 2022, Shifts 2.0 MS lesions, BraTS 2023 adult glioma and BraTS-Africa, OASIS-3, OASIS-4 and M4Raw 0.3 T into BIDS, recording MRIQC metrics (CJV, CNR, EFC, SNR) for every input. Released weights will be evaluated frozen with a light decoder, few-shot fine-tuned at 5–100 labelled cases, and zero-shot with oracle versus automatic prompts, alongside nnU-Net trained from scratch at the same budgets and SynthSeg and FastSurfer as no-training references, on fixed subject-level splits never used for selection. Controlled noise, k-space motion, bias field, downsampling and ghosting give dose-response curves validated against real MR-ART motion. The primary endpoint is the subject-level paired Dice difference from nnU-Net at 50 cases within quality tertiles, by Wilcoxon signed-rank with Holm correction and 2,000 subject bootstraps; a mixed model with subject and dataset intercepts estimates quality slopes; TOST margins are 0.02 Dice for anatomy and 0.05 for lesions; crossing points come from learning-curve fits. Pretraining overlap will be ledgered and seen data analysed separately; label-shuffled curves and an untrained-encoder probe give the floor.

**Expected results.** We expect frozen foundation models to lose at least 0.10 Dice per SD of worsening CJV against at most 0.05 for SynthSeg, larger motion-induced cortical volume bias, Dice drops of at least 0.15 next to stroke lesions and 0.10 on BraTS-Africa relative to adult BraTS, nnU-Net parity at roughly 25–50 lesion and 10–25 anatomy cases, deficits of 0.05–0.10 outside the pretraining age range, and at least 0.10 Dice lost when oracle prompts are replaced by automatic ones.

**Significance.** Either outcome is decision-relevant for hospitals weighing adoption, and the released harness, predictions and quality-tertile leaderboard let any future model be added and stratified rather than summarised by one mean Dice.
