# Batch-Corrected, Phylogenetically Aware Dendritic Scaling Laws Across the NeuroMorpho.Org Corpus

**Background.** Wiring-optimality theory predicts that total dendritic length scales with branch-point number and spanning volume as a power law with a 2/3 exponent for three-dimensional arbors and 1/2 for planar ones, confirmed on about 6,000 curated reconstructions. Such laws now serve as priors for synthetic morphology generators, cross-species claims about human neurons and neuromorphic design. Yet the original tests did not model the tracing laboratory as a random effect, two-species comparisons are largely single-consortium so lab and species are confounded, no neuromorphology study has used phylogenetic comparative methods, and the effective dimensionality of a reconstruction, which sets the predicted exponent, is itself a protocol property.

**Objective.** We will estimate allometric exponents across the full NeuroMorpho.Org corpus with archive as a random effect and software, shrinkage correction, protocol and slice thickness as covariates, quantify how much apparent between-species variance is absorbed by provenance, and test for phylogenetic signal in batch-adjusted species residuals.

**Methods.** We will harvest metadata and L-Measure morphometry for more than 250,000 reconstructions from roughly 1,000 laboratories and over 100 species, restrict to complete dendritic reconstructions with at least 20 branch points, remove duplicates by neuron name and PMID, and recompute length, branch points, convex-hull volume, Sholl profiles and PCA-based effective dimensionality from CNG SWC files, checked against L-Measure by Bland–Altman on a 20,000-cell sample. Mixed models of log length on log branch points, log volume, dimensionality, batch covariates, species, region and class with an archive random intercept yield exponents tested against theory by Wald and equivalence tests (±0.05), with cluster bootstraps by archive and leave-lab-out validation. Species attenuation is one minus the ratio of adjusted to naive species-effect variance, identified by multi-species labs and multi-lab species, with a null permuting species labels across archives. Batch-adjusted species means will be regressed on brain mass under a TimeTree-derived Brownian covariance by weighted PGLS with Pagel's lambda, a tip-shuffle null and sampling over divergence-time uncertainty. Region-by-class, axon and within-lab software contrasts are BH-corrected; hull definition and shrinkage exclusion are sensitivity axes.

**Expected results.** We expect pooled exponents to recover 2/3 and 1/3 for three-dimensional arbors and 1/2 and 1/2 for near-planar ones, at least 30% of naive between-species variance in length, branch count and Sholl peak to be absorbed by provenance, lambda above zero with a PGLS slope differing from OLS, conserved exponents for principal cells with deviations for interneurons and axons, and reduced z-extent in shrinkage-uncorrected reconstructions.

**Significance.** A citable table of provenance bias magnitudes and a corpus-wide batch-adjusted morphometrics release would let cross-species claims be stated net of who traced the cell.
