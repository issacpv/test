# How far can adult-trained 12-lead ECG models be pushed into childhood? Age-continuous generalization decay, shift decomposition and label-efficient adaptation on ZZU-pECG

**Background.** The pediatric ECG is not a small adult ECG: rates, intervals, amplitudes and T-wave polarity change from neonate to adolescent, so adult-trained models face covariate shift and an age-dependent waveform-to-label mapping. Adult-pretrained models reach about 59% macro-AUC zero-shot on ZZU-pECG, 82% with 50 labels and 92% fully fine-tuned; but no study charts where transfer breaks as a continuous function of age, decomposes the loss into covariate, prior and concept shift, or tests whether physiological age normalisation can replace fine-tuning across a fine label grid.

**Objective.** We will chart the age-resolved generalization decay of adult-trained ECG models on the open ZZU-pECG database, decompose the infant-versus-adolescent gap, and benchmark age-normalising adaptation against fine-tuning on a fine label grid, using labels comparable across age.

**Methods.** Adult sources are PTB-XL (21,799 ECGs), CODE-15% (345,779) and the PhysioNet/CinC 2021 training set (about 88k); the target is the 12-lead subset of ZZU-pECG (12,334 records, 11,643 children aged 0 to 14). Labels are harmonised to shared rhythm and conduction classes (sinus tachycardia and bradycardia, bundle branch block, first-degree AV block, ectopy). A 1D-ResNet and a handcrafted-feature baseline trained only on adults are evaluated zero-shot; AUROC is fitted as a smooth function of age with bootstrap bands, an age-permutation null and a matched-rate placebo subsampling adolescents to the infant rate distribution. The infant-adolescent gap is Shapley-decomposed using renormalisation to Rijnbeek age norms, reweighting and recalibration counterfactuals. Recalibration, age-conditioned standardisation, linear probe and full fine-tuning are compared on log-spaced label budgets against a pediatric-only model, with child-level disjoint splits, normalisation fitted on source or pediatric-train only and Benjamini-Hochberg correction.

**Expected results.** We expect AUROC to decline monotonically toward younger ages, worst in infants; covariate renormalisation to remove most of the gap for rate-defined labels, leaving a concept-shift gap for morphology labels; age-conditioned normalisation with a linear probe to reach within 2 AUROC points of the pediatric-only ceiling most label-efficiently, with full fine-tuning winning only beyond 2 to 3k labels; and confident errors spiking in infants for repolarisation outputs.

**Significance.** The decay curve and label-efficiency results tell hospitals with few pediatric ECGs which adult models can be reused, at what ages and at what cost.
