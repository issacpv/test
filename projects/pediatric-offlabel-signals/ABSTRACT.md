# Machine-readable labelled age floors turn FAERS into an age-off-label pharmacovigilance system for children

**Background.** Off-label prescribing to children is common and linked to adverse reactions in hospital cohorts, and a national VigiBase series shows reporter-flagged off-label reports to be more often serious; but FAERS does not code on-label status, so US studies infer it informally, drug by drug, and none has defined it from labelled age ranges. Yet onset age is recorded for most paediatric reports, and every SPL states its paediatric age floor in stereotyped phrasings. Hundreds of BPCA/PREA labelling changes, each converting an off-label age range into a labelled one, are natural experiments never evaluated with reporting data.

**Objective.** We will build a machine-readable age-floor resource from SPL text, classify every paediatric FAERS report as on-label-age, below-floor or unlabelled, and test whether age-off-label reports carry different, more often unlabelled, events and whether labelling changes shift reporting.

**Methods.** From openFDA drug/label (about 50k prescription labels) a regex extractor derives the minimum established age from pediatric_use and indications text, validated against 300 hand-labelled labels and the FDA Pediatric Labeling Changes table (900 rows). Paediatric FAERS reports 2015 to 2026 (1.5 million) are classified per suspect drug. Per drug and preferred term we compute Mantel-Haenszel reporting odds ratios within 1-year age strata for below-floor and on-label reports and their ratio, Benjamini-Hochberg across terms, and enrichment in medication-error and unlabelled terms. Seriousness and death are modelled by logistic regression with 1-year age, sex, reporter type and drug effects. Segmented Poisson interrupted time series 36 months around each floor-lowering change are pooled by random-effects meta-analysis. Nulls are permuted label class within age-by-drug strata, placebo change dates and negative-control drugs.

**Expected results.** We expect at least 90 percent exact agreement of extracted floors with hand labels; 25 to 40 percent of paediatric reports to be below-floor or unlabelled, highest in infants; age-adjusted odds of serious outcome of at least 1.3 for below-floor reports; at least 10 percent of drugs to show a below-floor excess term enriched for dosing errors and unlabelled events; a fall in below-floor share after labelling changes; and a downward bias of at least 5 points when reports with only an age group are excluded.

**Significance.** The floor table is a reusable public resource, and the design lets regulators monitor age-off-label harm at scale and measure what labelling changes achieve.
