# Why preoperative risk models fail to travel: shift decomposition, calibration-first evaluation and a cohort-definition multiverse across INSPIRE, MOVER and the MIMIC-IV surgical cohort

**Background.** Preoperative risk models reach AUROC 0.85 to 0.95 inside one health system. A bidirectional INSPIRE-to-MOVER mortality study has already shown direction-asymmetric transfer, within-stratum AUROC far below the aggregate, calibration slopes of 0.41 to 1.29 before Platt scaling, and better travel for intraoperative than preoperative features. It covers one outcome and two sites, attributes failure only by case-mix matching, does not separate covariate, label and concept shift, and says nothing about how many labelled encounters a receiving hospital needs; VitalDB and INSPIRE are still treated as separate sources although both come from Seoul National University Hospital.

**Objective.** We will add a third, differently coded site, decompose transfer failure into covariate, label and concept shift for three outcomes, quantify the labelled sample a hospital needs to recalibrate a foreign model, and show how much the surgical-cohort definition changes the answer.

**Methods.** Sites are INSPIRE (Korea), MOVER (Irvine) and MIMIC-IV v3.1 (Boston), whose surgical cohort is defined six ways from PACU transfers, ICD procedure codes and surgical services. Outcomes are 30-day in-hospital mortality, unplanned ICU admission within 24 h and KDIGO AKI within 7 days. Logistic regression and LightGBM on a harmonised preoperative ontology (12 labs, demographics, emergency flag, surgery group, Charlson) are tuned by subject-grouped cross-validation and evaluated on all six site pairs. Covariate shift is estimated by importance weighting, label shift by black-box shift estimation and concept shift as the remainder, Shapley-averaged with 1,000 subject-level bootstraps; random halves of one site must give components near zero and synthetic label shift must be recovered. Calibration intercept, slope and net benefit are reported with few-shot recalibration curves for 50 to 5,000 target encounters and sex and age subgroups; 54 tests are Benjamini-Hochberg controlled, and INSPIRE year strata give within-site temporal comparisons.

**Expected results.** We expect label shift to dominate intercept error and concept shift discrimination loss for mortality, concept shift to dominate ICU admission and creatinine-availability covariate shift to dominate AKI; slopes below 1 with intercept error tracking log prevalence ratio; 200 target encounters to remove at least 80 percent of intercept error; transported AUROC to vary by at least 0.05 across MIMIC-IV cohort definitions; and within-INSPIRE temporal drift to be at least half the geographic gap.

**Significance.** The results turn external validation into a recipe: what fails, why, and what a receiving hospital must label before deployment.
