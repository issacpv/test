# Not Missing at Random: A Multi-Source Audit of Differential Label Noise in Report-Derived Chest Radiograph Labels and Its Effect on Benchmarks and Fairness Claims

**Background.** Chest radiograph AI is trained and evaluated on labels extracted from reports by CheXpert, NegBio, CheXbert and LLMs. Against radiologist re-reads of the reports these labelers are accurate but err more in older patients; against the images, small re-reads show frequent disagreement, and a cardiomegaly case study attributed the gap mainly to non-mention rather than negation. Whether image-level disagreement is differential by acquisition, setting and demographics, and how much of published rankings and underdiagnosis gaps it generates, is unmeasured.

**Objective.** We will build per-finding, per-labeler noise matrices against expert image labels, test whether noise is differential by view, portable acquisition, care setting and demographics, and quantify its consequences for fairness metrics and model rankings by calibrated simulation and re-scoring.

**Methods.** Expert labels from Chest ImaGenome (500 gold studies), REFLACX (about 3,000 images) and MS-CXR on MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert validation and test sets (700 studies), VinDr-CXR (18,000 triple-read images) and the NIH adjudicated subset (about 4,000 images) are mapped to the 14 CheXpert findings. Reports are labelled with CheXpert, NegBio, CheXbert, a transparent rule-based labeler and an on-premises LLM labeler with a frozen prompt. Sensitivity, specificity, kappa and PABAK are estimated under both non-mention conventions with clustered bootstrap CIs. Differential noise is modelled by logistic regression of disagreement on view, setting, demographics and report-structure features with cluster-robust errors, Holm correction, E-values and within-setting permutation of subgroup labels as null. Measured subgroup noise rates are injected into true labels to compute the false-negative-rate gap a perfect classifier would display. At least five public models, screened for training overlap, are scored on expert versus report labels with Kendall tau.

**Expected results.** We expect sensitivity below 0.6 for cardiomegaly, atelectasis and effusion with specificity above 0.9; odds of non-mention false negatives above 2 for ICU portable films and above 3 for comparison-style reports, with adjusted demographic odds ratios near 1; simulations reproducing 30-60% of reported no-finding gaps; rankings with tau below 0.7 for at least three findings; and LLM labelers cutting negation errors without changing the differential structure.

**Significance.** The audit yields reusable noise matrices, a noise-injection simulator and a checklist separating label artefacts from model bias in weak-label benchmarks.
