# Does Synthetic EHR Training Data Transport? Decomposing the Synthetic Gap and the Site Gap from MIMIC-IV to eICU Across Rule-Based, Copula, GAN and Diffusion Generators

**Background.** Synthetic electronic health records are promoted for sharing, teaching and prototyping without privacy risk, and their utility is almost always scored by training on synthetic data and testing on a held-out slice of the same real database. That answers whether the source distribution is preserved, not whether a model built on synthetic data survives deployment at another hospital. Existing benchmarks evaluate single databases or same-hospital cross-era transfer, include no rule-based generator, and do not decompose transport loss. Learned generators also copy the source's measurement process (which labs are ordered, when, and how missingness looks), the features known to drive cross-site shift in ICU models, so they may yield models that transport worse than the real source data would.

**Objective.** We will test whether synthetic training data amplifies cross-site performance loss, attribute any amplification to copied observation-process features, and determine how many real target records restore parity.

**Methods.** A 2x2 design crosses training source (real MIMIC-IV versus synthetic) with evaluation site (MIMIC-IV holdout versus eICU-CRD, 208 hospitals, about 200,000 ICU stays) for in-hospital mortality and 30-day readmission on a shared 26-concept encounter ontology including per-lab measurement counts and missingness indicators. Generators span Synthea (rule-based, never sees MIMIC), a Gaussian copula, CTGAN, TVAE and tabular diffusion, plus independent-marginals negative and bootstrap-jitter positive controls, five seeds each. MIMIC-IV patients are split 70/30 by subject; eICU is used only for evaluation except a disjoint recalibration sample. Logistic regression and gradient boosting are scored by AUROC, AUPRC, Brier, calibration intercept and slope, and ECE; the synthetic gap, site gap and their interaction (amplification) carry 1,000-resample stay-level bootstrap intervals, and a split-source null must give a site gap near zero. Analyses are repeated without the observation-process block, by sex, age band and race, across the ten largest eICU hospitals, and with intercept-slope recalibration on 100 to 2,500 target records. Fidelity (KS, pMSE, MMD) and privacy (distance to closest record, membership inference) are reported alongside.

**Expected results.** We expect positive amplification for learned generators in AUROC and calibration-in-the-large with intervals excluding zero, a smaller site gap but larger synthetic gap for Synthea, at least a 50% reduction in amplification without observation-process features, fidelity metrics that track source but not target utility, larger subgroup gaps for synthetic-trained models, and 500 target records closing most of the calibration gap but not the discrimination gap.

**Significance.** The results would show that current fidelity metrics cannot certify synthetic data for model building at a new site, and provide a transport-aware evaluation design for future benchmarks.
