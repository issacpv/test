# Gain or Shape? A Pooled Subject-Level Re-Analysis of Individual Electric Field and Behavioural Response to Transcranial Electrical Stimulation Under Montage and Spatial Null Models

**Background.** Transcranial electrical stimulation delivers a fixed 1-2 mA current regardless of head anatomy, yet finite-element models show two- to three-fold inter-individual variability in cortical electric field (E-field) for the same montage, so many participants may receive sub-threshold fields. Individual E-field has been linked to physiological after-effects and clinical outcomes in single studies, and demographic predictors of peak E-field are now established for dose standardisation. Two problems remain. E-field maps from one montage are strongly spatially autocorrelated and nearly identical in shape across subjects, differing mainly in gain, so vertex-wise E-field-to-behaviour maps evaluated by parametric or naive permutation tests can be a montage artefact, and no such analysis has used spatial null models. Single studies of 10-40 participants also cannot separate whether dose magnitude or field shape drives behaviour.

**Objective.** We will pool subject-level E-field and behavioural data across independent open tDCS and tACS datasets to test whether individual target E-field predicts behavioural response, whether shape adds anything beyond global gain, and how many map-level findings survive spatial and montage nulls.

**Methods.** A programmatic OpenNeuro registry will identify tES datasets with a behavioural outcome and, where present, a T1-weighted image (for example ds003670 and ds006126); montages will be hand-verified and ROI definitions, sign conventions and models pre-registered on OSF before any simulation. SimNIBS 4 head models will be built per subject, or the MNI152 template for datasets without T1 (flagged separately), simulated at 1 mA and scaled linearly to the applied current. Dose metrics will include target ROI mean and normal E-field, 99th percentile magnitude, focality and global grey-matter mean magnitude (gain). Behavioural effects (active minus sham, standardised and sign-aligned) will be modelled per dataset on dose with age and sex, pooled by DerSimonian-Laird random effects with I-squared and prediction intervals, and leave-one-dataset-out sensitivity. Nested mixed models will test target dose against gain plus residual shape. Vertex-wise correlation maps on fsaverage will be evaluated with 5,000 spin rotations and 5,000 subject-label permutations that keep each subject's map intact, using a max-statistic over nulls, with equivalence tests at a smallest effect of interest of r = 0.2.

**Expected results.** With 150-250 pooled subjects we have 80% power for r of 0.2-0.25. We expect a positive pooled dose slope, no added value of residual shape once gain is removed, and fewer than 25% of naively significant vertices surviving spatial and montage nulls.

**Significance.** The study will either establish E-field gain as the usable dose variable for individualised tES or show that E-field-behaviour correlations are null-model artefacts; both outcomes change how dose-controlled stimulation is justified.
