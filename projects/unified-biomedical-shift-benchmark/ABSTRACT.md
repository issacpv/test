# BioShift: a WILDS-style distribution-shift benchmark with typed shift axes across ICU, ECG, EEG and echocardiography

**Background.** WILDS showed that typed, realistic shifts change conclusions about robustness methods, but its one clinical dataset is histopathology. Clinical shift benchmarks are single-modality (BEDS-Bench, TableShift, YAIB, ManyDG) and foundation-model benchmarks report transfer without typing the shift or reporting calibration uniformly. Claims that calibration fails first, that population shift costs more than device shift, that embedding diagnostics predict loss and that no robustness method beats ERM have never been tested across modalities under one protocol.

**Objective.** To build one task, split, metric and diagnostic interface across four modalities and test these claims with modality as a random effect.

**Methods.** A frozen registry of 6 tasks, 17 domains and 69 cells (36 ICU across MIMIC-IV, eICU-CRD, HiRID and AmsterdamUMCdb; 25 ECG across PTB-XL, Chapman, Ningbo, Georgia, CPSC and CODE-15%; 5 EEG across CHB-MIT, Siena, TUSZ and Helsinki; 3 echo across EchoNet-Dynamic, EchoNet-Pediatric and CAMUS) tags each cell with site, population, device, label-protocol and temporal axes and contamination flags. Every cell yields the same row: in-domain and transfer metric with group-bootstrap CIs, calibration intercept and slope, subgroup gaps, domain-classifier AUC, MMD and a covariate/label/concept decomposition. Group-level split manifests are released; no target labels touch training outside a recalibration arm disjoint from evaluation. Because the scope spans four data-access processes, the benchmark is modular: the harness already runs on synthetic domains, the open ECG and EEG cells form a public subset released first, and credentialed ICU and echo cells follow from the same manifests after pre-registration. Controls are group-disjoint halves of one domain and synthetic cells with known shift. Hypotheses are tested with mixed models on standardised gaps with modality random intercepts and slopes, Benjamini-Hochberg across six families; the sweep compares importance weighting, group DRO and recalibration against ERM by paired bootstrap.

**Expected results.** We expect calibration-in-the-large shifts outside the in-domain CI in >= 75% of cells while discrimination holds, larger drops for population-tagged cells, Spearman rho >= 0.5 between domain-classifier AUC and loss, subgroup gaps widening in >= 60% of cells, no method beating ERM in more than two modalities, and smaller foundation-model gaps on contaminated cells.

**Significance.** The benchmark makes shift type a first-class variable for clinical ML.
