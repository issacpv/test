# A Database-Wide Map of Unlabeled FAERS Signals and the Lag from Signal Emergence to US Label Update, Benchmarked Against FDA's Quarterly Potential Signals

**Background.** Post-marketing signal detection in FAERS exists to find risks not yet on the label, yet standard disproportionality workflows do not know what the label says, so already-labelled reactions dominate published signals and new ones are buried. The time between when a signal became detectable and when the label named it is rarely measured; audits of FDA's 603 potential signals from 2008 to 2019 start the clock at FDA's listing rather than at emergence. Automated extraction of labelled reactions from Structured Product Labels is now feasible, but no study has joined a validated labelled-reaction set to a dated FAERS scan across all drugs.

**Objective.** We will build a database-wide unlabeled-signal map for US prescription ingredients, date the emergence of every sustained signal, measure the lag to the first label version naming it, and evaluate the scan against FDA's own quarterly potential-signals list.

**Methods.** Current prescription labels from openFDA (about 45,000 set ids) will be parsed by section and affirmed adverse-reaction terms mapped to MedDRA preferred terms with a dictionary matcher handling negation and hierarchy, validated against 100 manually annotated labels (two annotators) and the 200-label Demner-Fushman corpus. FAERS data from 2004Q1 to 2025Q2 (about 20 million reports, suspect drugs only) will yield cumulative quarterly 2x2 tables for roughly 600 ingredients with at least 1,000 reports; a sustained signal requires IC025 above 0 with at least three reports for two consecutive quarters, with ROR and PRR as sensitivity criteria, BH-FDR q-values, and false-emergence rates calibrated on OMOP and EU-ADR negative controls. Signals are flagged unlabeled after exact, synonym and HLT-sibling matching. For labelled pairs, the first label version containing the term will be located in DailyMed version history and the FDA Safety-related Labeling Changes database; lags will be analysed by Kaplan-Meier with right censoring, label-first pairs excluded, and Cox regression clustered by drug. Detection rate, lead time and a precision proxy will be computed against FDA entries from 2008 to 2025 with thresholds fixed a priori.

**Expected results.** We expect at least 25% of sustained signals to be unlabeled, more for drugs approved over 15 years ago; a median signal-to-label lag above 24 months, shorter for designated medical events; over 30% of signals for labelled events to emerge only after labelling, quantifying stimulated reporting; detection of at least 60% of FDA entries with a median lead of two or more quarters; and extractor F1 of at least 0.85.

**Significance.** The study provides a public, quarterly-updated unlabeled-signal map and a reproducible benchmark for scoring future signal-detection methods.
