# Multi-Site, Multi-Modal Validation of Connectome-Based Whole-Mouse-Brain Models Against 17-Site Resting-State fMRI and Widefield Calcium Imaging Under Spatially Informed Nulls

**Background.** Virtual-Mouse-Brain models build whole-brain dynamics from the Allen mesoscale connectome and have been validated against functional connectivity (FC) from one or two laboratories, usually under anaesthesia and with a single connectome construction. Mouse resting-state fMRI is now a multi-centre measurement with standardised preprocessing, and widefield calcium imaging offers an awake, direct cortical readout in the same Common Coordinate Framework. Yet no study has asked whether the model fit is a property of the model or of one lab's acquisition, whether models that fit BOLD FC also fit calcium FC, how much of the connectome's explanatory power is reproduced by spatial embedding alone, or which connectome construction choices matter for simulated dynamics.

**Objective.** We will fit one family of connectome-based models across 17 fMRI sites and awake widefield sessions, benchmark fits against degree- and distance-preserving null connectomes, and quantify how connectome construction and dynamical model choice each affect empirical fit.

**Methods.** Connectomes will be built from the Allen Mouse Brain Connectivity Atlas as a named multiverse: normalised connection density versus strength, ipsilateral and contralateral handling, symmetrisation, density thresholds of 5-50%, log transform, and a MouseLight single-axon alternative. Empirical FC will come from OpenNeuro ds001720 (255 subjects, 17 sites, multiple anaesthesia protocols) preprocessed with RABIES to CCF space and parcellated to the same regions, and from resting widefield GCaMP sessions on DANDI registered to the Allen dorsal map, with FC from 0.01-0.1 Hz band-passed series and FC dynamics (FCD) from sliding windows. Models are a linear stochastic model with analytic Lyapunov covariance, Hopf oscillators simulated by Euler-Maruyama, and optionally Wilson-Cowan, passed through a Balloon-Windkessel transform for BOLD or a calcium kernel for widefield. Global coupling will be selected leave-one-site-out and leave-one-session-out so no parameter is tuned on scored data. Degree-preserving rewiring, distance-preserving weight permutation and random shuffles (500 realisations each) will be simulated through the same pipeline; site effects enter mixed models on temporal SNR, connectome rankings are compared by Kendall's tau, multiverse variance is decomposed by ANOVA, and FCD fit uses Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance.

**Expected results.** We expect the site-selected coupling to transfer with fit loss under 0.05 in at least 12 of 17 sites; widefield-tuned models to predict BOLD FC above the spatial null with connectome rankings concordant across modalities (tau above 0.5); the distance-preserving null to reproduce at least 30% of explained FC variance, with tracer-specific gains concentrated in long-range and homotopic edges; connectome construction to explain more fit variance than model choice; and only nonlinear models to reproduce awake FCD.

**Significance.** The study delivers a public benchmark table of connectome by model by site and settles how much mouse structure-function modelling rests on acquisition, construction and geometry.
