# Do Growing White-Matter Hyperintensities Amplify Amyloid's Effect on Cognitive Decline? A Time-Varying, Additive-Scale and Segmentation-Robust Test in OASIS-3 with ADNI Replication

**Background.** Cerebral small-vessel disease, visible as white-matter hyperintensities (WMH) on FLAIR, co-occurs with amyloid pathology in most older adults, and whether the two act additively or synergistically determines whether vascular prevention alters the course of amyloid-positive individuals. Existing evidence relies on baseline WMH entered as product terms in linear or logistic models, with recent work in cohorts of a few hundred reporting interactive contributions to decline. No study has modelled the concurrent WMH trajectory as a time-varying modifier of amyloid's effect on cognition and clinical progression, reported interaction on the additive scale with confidence intervals, or shown that the estimate survives the segmentation tool and the 1.5T to 3T protocol change. OASIS-3 holds FLAIR in a large fraction of its roughly 2,842 MR sessions with Centiloids, annual CDR and psychometrics over up to 15 years, but distributes no WMH table.

**Objective.** We will test whether WMH level and growth modify amyloid's effect on cognitive slopes and CDR progression in OASIS-3, quantify the interaction on multiplicative and additive scales, localise it to periventricular, deep and posterior compartments, and replicate in ADNI.

**Methods.** FLAIR sessions from about 1,378 OASIS-3 participants will be matched to amyloid PET within 365 days (PiB 16.4 and AV45 20.6 Centiloid thresholds, plus a continuous arm) and clinical visits within 180 days. WMH will be segmented with LST-LPA, BIANCA, SAMSEG-lesion and a deep-learning tool on the same FLAIR and T1, calibrated on the 60 manual masks of the MICCAI WMH challenge, split into periventricular and deep compartments by distance to the ventricles, and given lobar and posterior fractions; tool agreement is quantified by Dice, ICC and Bland-Altman. Linear mixed models with random slopes will estimate the time by amyloid by WMH(t) term for a global composite, episodic memory and CDR-SB, adjusting for age, sex, education, APOE e4 and scanner generation; person-period discrete-time hazard models for CDR 0 to 0.5 or more progression will yield RERI, attributable proportion and synergy index with 2,000-resample subject bootstrap intervals; joint longitudinal-survival models will link subject-specific WMH trajectories to hazard, and counterfactual mediation will test amyloid to WMH growth to decline with temporal ordering. A pre-registered primary specification sits within a specification curve over four tools, two compartment rules, binary or continuous amyloid and three outcomes. Nulls include amyloid permutation within age-sex strata and simulated cohorts with zero and known interaction. ADNI (about 1,500 with UC Davis WMH volumes, Centiloids and CDR-SB) is used only after the OASIS-3 analysis is frozen.

**Expected results.** We expect a negative three-way coefficient for cognition concentrated in episodic memory; RERI of 0.5 to 1.5 with attributable proportion of 20 to 40%; WMH growth as the stronger modifier, with baseline WMH underestimating the interaction by at least 30%; interaction carried by periventricular and posterior WMH; mediation under 10% of the total effect; and interaction coefficients within each other's intervals across tools and eras.

**Significance.** The results would establish whether vascular burden multiplies amyloid's harm, on the scale relevant to prevention, and release a multi-tool OASIS-3 WMH table.
